Diwan-i-Aam

ARCHITECTURAL 17TH CENTURY
graphic_eq RECORDING // 04A
The hall of public audience stands as a testament to imperial geometry. Note the scalloped arches, designed not just for structural integrity, but to frame the emperor as a living icon. The red sandstone, quarried from Fatehpur Sikri, retains the warmth of the sun long into the evening.
LIVING TRADITION

The Jharokha

The marble canopy where the emperor appeared daily is a direct translation of the Hindu tradition of 'darshan'—auspicious sight.
